About this one

Two details make this kettle different from the one in most kitchens. The lid is released by a button on top of the handle and springs fully open, so it can be filled and closed one-handed. And the water window runs down **both** sides, so you can read the level whichever hand you are holding it in.

It holds 1.7 litres, which is about seven mugs, and the minimum fill is 0.5 litres. Boiling a full kettle takes roughly four and a half minutes on a standard 120-volt supply. It sits on a 360-degree cordless base, so it lifts off from any angle, and the 30-inch cord stows in a channel underneath.

The handle is 1.4 inches across at the grip, set well back from the body so steam from the spout does not cross it. It has a concealed element — the heating element is under the stainless floor rather than a coil in the water — which means there is nothing to descale around and the inside wipes clean.

It switches off automatically when the water boils and has a dry-boil cutout. Filter in the spout is removable and rinsable.

  • How to look after it

    • Unplug and let it cool before cleaning. Never immerse the kettle or the base in water.
    • Wipe the outside with a damp cloth. Wipe the inside out; do not scour the stainless floor.
    • Descale every one to three months depending on your water, with a proprietary descaler or a half-and-half white vinegar and water solution, then boil and discard two full kettles of fresh water.
    • Rinse the spout filter under a tap when it looks furred.
    • Do not fill above the maximum mark; a kettle filled past MAX can spit boiling water from the spout.
