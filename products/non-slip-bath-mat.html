About this one

A bath mat with a rubber backing rather than a plain hemmed one. The backing is a sprayed natural latex applied in a dotted pattern over the whole underside, which grips a tiled or vinyl floor and lets the mat breathe and dry.

**That is a description of the material, not a promise about what will happen.** How a mat behaves depends on your floor, whether it is wet, and what is on your feet. We will tell you what the backing is made of and how it is applied; we are not going to make a safety claim, because no shop honestly can.

The pile is 1,200gsm long-staple cotton at a 0.6-inch pile height — thick enough to absorb a wet foot and dry out again before the next person. It is 20 by 32 inches, which is the size that actually covers the front of a tub rather than sitting in the middle of the floor.

It is machine washable, and this is the thing most people get wrong with rubber-backed mats: wash warm and **tumble dry on low or line dry**. A hot dryer cracks natural latex, and once the backing has cracked it stops gripping and starts shedding crumbs.

Everything we sell is a household convenience product. It is not a medical device, a mobility aid or a safety device, and we make no claim that it prevents, treats or improves anything. What is on this page is the material, the measurements and what is in the box.

  • What is in the box

  • How to look after it

    • Machine wash warm, on its own or with towels. Wash before first use.
    • **Tumble dry on low, or line dry.** A hot dryer cracks the latex backing.
    • Do not bleach and do not use fabric softener; softener coats the pile and stops it absorbing.
    • Do not iron and do not dry clean.
    • Hang it up between uses so it dries. A mat left flat on a wet floor stays damp underneath.
